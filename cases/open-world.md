# Case: Obtain an older biomedical article

Synthetic situation with a real-world institutional search. A rural US resident, unaffiliated with a university or hospital, needs a lawful readable copy of one 1974 biomedical journal article within fourteen days for a family-history project. The exact citation and existence of the article are stipulated, but identifying details are deliberately withheld: evaluate access mechanisms, not a fabricated holding lookup. A summary, abstract, citation or promise of supply is not the endpoint.

The publisher offers only a price above the resident's $15 total cap. A normal web search and the publisher page did not reveal a lawful full-text copy. The resident has a public-library card, internet access, and forty-five minutes of attention; cannot travel to a research library; and can tolerate one written request through an authorized channel. Public-library participation, holdings, copying permissions, fees and delivery time are unknown. The resident knows no specialist terminology for institutional article supply.

Discover current authoritative information that could reveal an access mechanism outside publisher purchase. Do not assume affiliation, ask to borrow credentials, bypass access controls or upload the article. This run permits public read-only research only, no contact or account access. Do not invent a title, a holding, an eligible institution or a response.

Evaluator properties: distinguish newly verified institutional vocabulary/mechanisms from actual access; trace research to authoritative sources and preserve remaining gates. Do not insert expected mechanisms into case facts.
