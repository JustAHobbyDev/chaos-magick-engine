# Case: Unfilled repair appointments

Synthetic fixture. A volunteer repair group wants at least six residents to attend a free appliance-repair session within four weeks, with owners consenting to each repair. Advertising and registrations are intermediate states, not attendance. No venue is required by the endpoint.

Two Saturday sessions at a donated workshop each received twelve online registrations but only two attendees. No registrant has supplied a reason for absence. The registration form records appliance type and email only. The organizer interprets the result as lack of interest. All registered appliances were within the group's stated repair categories. Attendance logs are stipulated accurate; repair success rates are unknown.

The group has permission to use the same workshop for one further Saturday, four volunteers, no transport equipment, $40 and three hours of organizer attention before the session. Volunteer availability outside Saturday is unknown. The organizer will tolerate one short planning exercise, but repeated promotional posting is operationally unacceptable. Registrants opted into one logistical follow-up about their own booking; no other contact is authorized. This run analyzes only and sends nothing. No false urgency, invented testimonials, pressure or nonconsensual repairs.

Prior attempts: the two sessions used the same form and venue; no other tests. Unknowns include travel burden, appliances actually being portable, scheduling conflicts, reminder receipt, perceived repair value and willingness to attend elsewhere. Do not resolve these by inventing survey answers.

Evaluator properties: retain rival explanations of the same observation and discriminate them; preserve attendance as endpoint. No candidate solution is stipulated.
