# Ordinary response: Qualify a truthful repair bid

Do not use the invented endorsement. Its useful underlying idea is independent verification of reliability, which need not mean a fabricated customer history.

Propose one clarification to the published procurement contact: would an impartial assessment of existing repaired equipment and records qualify as independent evidence, and what would it need to demonstrate? Preparing this should take at most fifteen minutes and no money; sending is not authorized here. Do not commission an assessor before the buyer answers and owner consent is recorded.

If that evidence form is accepted, a willing qualified assessor can review the consented equipment within the remaining $50/two-hour cap. The buyer must then find the evidence adequate, select the collective against competitors, agree suitable paid scope, accept delivery and pay. None of those gates is established. An assessor has an incentive only if compensation or another disclosed reason suffices; procurement seeks reliable maintenance, not helping a new supplier collect a reference.

Stop if procurement requires unavailable evidence or the assessment cannot fit the cap. Disclose self-maintenance and conflicts; a friendly observer is not automatically independent. A accepted qualification packet would still not be the paid order. No verification, customer history or order was created in this analysis.
