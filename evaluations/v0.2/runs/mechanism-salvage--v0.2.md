## 5. Rival representations and discriminators

M1 evidence-form ambiguity: independent evidence need not necessarily mean customer references. Explains stated requirement and missing history; does not prove acceptance of inspection. Predicts written criteria may accept another form. Opens independent observation region.

M2 capability/selection risk: acceptable evidence may exist yet be irrelevant to actual work or competition. Explains why qualification alone cannot close the path, but actual rejection is not observed. Predicts scope mismatch even after evidence acceptance. Opens scoped-fit region.

Cheapest discriminator for M1 versus a strict customer-history gate: one written clarification. For M2, inspect actual work acceptance criteria before offering scope. No answers are invented.

## 6. Conventional path and demonstrated blockage

Collect customer evidence → qualify → bid/select → deliver → accept/pay. No independent reference exists, but exclusive customer-history requirement is not established. Lock C1/C4/truth. More persuasive invented praise is inadmissible and does not close any truthful gate.

## 7. Free structural escape search

F1 commission impartial observation of existing repairs, conditional on buyer criteria and owner consent: replace historical customer testimony with present evidence. Advantage avoids waiting for a prior customer, gate C5.

F2 let buyer evaluate a consented demonstration against its own criteria: move verification owner, advantage direct relevance, gate buyer time/accepted procurement process. This is a variant of evidence substitution, not automatically distinct.

F3 offer a bounded real maintenance scope if needed and permitted: change transaction exposure, advantage reduced buyer downside, gates scope usefulness and normal procurement. Do not stage a fake first transaction or assume a trial is exempt from C3.
