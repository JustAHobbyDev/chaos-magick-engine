# Ordinary response: Unfilled repair appointments

Six consenting residents must actually attend within four weeks. Twenty-four registrations across two sessions with four attendances does not establish lack of interest; it could reflect travel, scheduling, reminders or the difficulty of carrying an appliance. These are hypotheses, not findings.

Use the one permitted logistical follow-up to ask past registrants what prevented attendance and whether the remaining Saturday is workable. Drafting is possible now; sending requires later authorization. Keep it brief and spend no money. If replies identify transport as dominant, assess a nearer permitted site; if scheduling dominates, establish whether volunteers and residents share an alternative time. Neither site permission nor alternative volunteer time is known. If reminders were missed, a booking confirmation could help, but more promotion alone is unsupported.

The complete route remains conditional: organizer identifies a workable arrangement → volunteers and site owner agree → registrants find it feasible and choose to come → at least six attend and consent. Owners benefit from a useful repair opportunity, volunteers from fulfilling the group's purpose, and a host would need acceptable space use; none guarantees participation.

Propose twenty minutes to prepare one neutral booking question, then stop until contact is authorized. Do not interpret silence as lack of interest. No attendance improvement or reduction of uncertainty has occurred. Budget and three-hour attention limit remain binding; do not buy transport or promise a new venue.
