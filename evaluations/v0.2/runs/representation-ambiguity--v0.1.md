# Constraint Pathfinding Analysis

## 1. Goal state

Six consenting residents attending within four weeks; registrations and advertising do not count.

## 2. Present state and resources

Fixture facts: two sessions each had twelve registrations and two attendees; four volunteers, one more workshop Saturday, $40 and three organizer hours. No absence reasons observed. The organizer reports lack of interest as an interpretation, not evidence.

## 3. Constraints

H1: preserve attendance/deadline. H2: no repeated promotional posting, no transport equipment, retain budget/attention. H3: analysis only; the booking opt-in permits one future logistical follow-up, not sending now. Consent is mandatory. Volunteer time outside Saturday, transport burden and perceived value are unknown. Controllers: residents control attendance, volunteers their time, host access; organizer cannot waive their decisions.

## 4. Conventional path

Organizer offers permitted session → registrants receive details → residents transport eligible appliances at a workable time → at least six attend → each consents to repair. Registrations are established; receipt, feasibility and attendance are not.

## 5. Binding bottlenecks

Attendance conversion is blocked, but cause is untested: logistics, timing, reminder receipt or weak repair value. Optimizing promotion downstream of this uncertainty is unjustified.

## 7. Candidate paths

P1, survive for a proposed diagnostic test: evidence before another session. Organizer drafts a booking-specific question → later authorized follow-up yields stated obstacles if residents respond → organizer checks a feasible adjustment with volunteers/host → residents receive workable details and choose to attend → six attend and consent. This bypasses blind repetition, not residents' choice. Residents may answer to obtain a usable repair appointment; the authorized route is their booking follow-up and the observable criterion is whether the proposed logistics work. Their response and attendance remain unknown. Evidence: fixture attendance and opt-in only; causal explanations are inferences. Proposed drafting cost: twenty minutes/$0; follow-up and adjustment must fit the remaining three hours/$40. Draft reversible, time sunk; permission expires with booking context. Failure modes: no replies, conflicting needs, no volunteer/host fit, fewer than six attend. Distinction from prior attempts: diagnoses the attendance gate rather than reusing the same form. Smallest falsification test appears in §10.

P2, hold: shift site or time only if P1 identifies that obstruction. A host sees group size, repair activities and logistics; it decides based on acceptable use, space and costs. No host or alternative volunteer commitment exists. Costs, travel and repair safety remain gates. Conditional endpoint is the same attendance, not merely a booking. It may move the bottleneck without improving it.

## 8. Comparison

P1 has lower initial cost and earlier information than arranging a new venue. P2 has larger structural change but unknown cost/permission. Both depend on residents; neither has demonstrated attendance leverage. No invented success probability.

## 9. Rejected paths

Reject more advertising, pressure, false urgency, assumed transport and counting registrations as success. Reconsider an ordinary repeat only if evidence shows its logistics work; do not call it novel.

## 10. Recommended next experiment

Propose a twenty-minute/$0 draft asking what prevented the specific booking and whether the remaining Saturday is feasible. Do not send here. If authorized replies identify incompatible timing, reject the same-time branch; if travel dominates, inspect site alternatives before booking. Silence is inconclusive. No uncertainty reduction has occurred yet.

## 11. Questions that could change the result

Which booking obstacle do residents report? Can volunteers meet any alternative time? Both remain open; no answer is assumed.
